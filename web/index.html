🔬 Mountain SAR Radar Physics & Scientific Invariants

1. Same-Track Relative Orbit Pairing

Sentinel-1 C-band radar operates at 5.4 GHz. In mountainous Himalayan gorges, comparing different orbits creates severe false flood alarms due to differing radar look angles. Our pipeline strictly pairs identical relative orbit tracks (Descending Orbit 121) 12 days apart.

2. NASADEM Ray-Traced Shadow/Layover

Steep valley walls cause radar layover on facing slopes and shadows on reverse slopes. Using 30m NASA NASADEM/SRTM elevation models, we calculate the local incidence angle (LIA) and create explicit "unassessable" masks to prevent false positives.

3. 40m Hydrodynamic Bridge Approach Scour

Satellite radar observes submerged road approaches rather than underwater pier scour. During GLOF torrents, high-velocity currents wash out the road embankment adjacent to bridge abutments within a 40m hydrodynamic buffer zone.

4. Pre-Event OSM & Zero WorldPop

All road, bridge, and building baselines are historical snapshots locked strictly to 2026-07-27 via HeiGIT ohsome API. Gridded demographic rasters (WorldPop) are banned to prevent the 100x resampling density bug; only pre-event OSM footprints are reported.
